The Kitchen Is Where It Starts

We clear more kitchen drains in Northeast Bellevue than any other single fixture, and the culprit is almost always the same: grease. Not the dramatic kind — nobody's pouring bacon fat down the sink anymore. It's the slow accumulation: the oily film rinsed off sheet pans, the butter scraped from plates, the cooking oil wiped from a skillet with a paper towel that still leaves residue. Hot water carries it all down the line, where it cools, congeals, and grabs onto every bit of food debris that follows.

Garbage disposals make it worse, not better. A disposal doesn't eliminate food waste; it just makes the pieces smaller so they travel further before they get stuck — usually in the horizontal run under the slab or in the wall, where they're much harder to reach. If your kitchen sink drains fine until the dishwasher runs, or backs up into the other basin, you've got a grease-narrowed line, and it needs jetting, not chemicals. Drain cleaner just sits on top of a grease clog and generates heat; it doesn't dissolve a six-foot coating of congealed oil.

Kid Bathrooms Take a Beating

The second-busiest drains in a family home are the kids' bathrooms, and they clog for entirely different reasons. Long hair is the obvious one — a household with two or three long-haired kids can felt a drain trap in months. But the surprise offenders are the things that shouldn't be in the toilet at all: "flushable" wipes (they aren't, not really), cotton balls, dental floss, and the occasional toy soldier or rubber duck that went on an adventure.

We pull remarkable things out of Northeast Bellevue bathroom drains. The fix is usually quick — a cable through the trap and branch line — but the prevention matters more. A five-dollar hair catcher in every shower drain and a trash can next to every toilet will prevent more service calls than any product on the hardware store shelf. And if you have a toddler, assume everything smaller than a tennis ball is a future clog until proven otherwise.

What a Family-Home Service Visit Looks Like

When we come out to a Northeast Bellevue home, we don't just clear the one drain that's complaining. Family plumbing problems travel in packs — the slow kitchen sink and the gurgling powder room are often the same developing main-line restriction showing up in two places. So we check the fixtures, run water through the system, and camera the main line when the symptoms point that way.

Then we fix what's actually wrong and tell you how to keep it from coming back: what not to put in the disposal (fibrous vegetables, coffee grounds, eggshells, and grease are the big four), how often to flush the kitchen line with truly hot water, and which bathroom habits are quietly building the next clog. When It's Time to Call

Call us when a plunger stops working, when two or more fixtures are slow at the same time, when drains gurgle, when there's a sewage smell anywhere in the house, or when the same drain has needed attention twice in a year. Those are the signs that the problem has outgrown DIY. And if water is actively backing up onto the floor, shut off the water to that fixture and call immediately — every minute of standing water is damage to floors and cabinets.
